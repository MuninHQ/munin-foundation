# Munin Problem Interview Guide

Purpose: test whether continuity/context reconstruction is a repeated problem worth solving. Do not pitch Munin before the behavioral questions are complete.

## Participant profile
Professionals balancing several active domains (career, family, learning, projects, finances or side work) across multiple tools and devices.

## Opening
- What are the main things you are actively managing right now?
- Which apps/tools do you use for them?

## Observed behavior
- Tell me about the last time you had to resume something after a few days or weeks away.
- What did you need to reconstruct before you could continue?
- Where did you look for that context?
- How long did that take?
- What did you get wrong or forget?
- How often does this happen?

## Workarounds
- What do you currently do to avoid losing context?
- Notes, reminders, chats, email flags, spreadsheets, bookmarks, screenshots, calendar, task managers?
- Which workaround fails most often and why?

## Cross-tool continuity
- Tell me about the last time relevant context was split across two or more tools.
- Did you manually copy anything between them?
- What happens when you switch device or assistant?

## Trust and correction
- What information would you allow a persistent assistant to remember?
- What would make you distrust it?
- How important is seeing where a remembered fact came from?
- If the assistant remembered something incorrectly, what would you expect to happen after correction?

## Proactivity and autonomy
- What recurring follow-up do you wish happened automatically?
- What actions should always require approval?
- What would be unacceptable for an assistant to do autonomously?

## Adoption test
Only now describe the concept neutrally: a personal continuity layer that preserves current state, provenance, corrections and resumable next actions across sessions/tools.

- What part is useful, if any?
- What existing product would you use instead?
- What would have to be true for you to try it for 30 days?
- What would make you stop using it?

## Evidence rules
Record concrete recent examples separately from opinions. A hypothetical desire is not equivalent to observed pain. Do not count a participant as positive merely because they like the concept.
